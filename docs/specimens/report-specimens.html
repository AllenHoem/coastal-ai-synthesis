01

Agreed — and the harness proves it rather than conceding it

Tokens per joule is not a forcing function for a buyer. But "I agree" is a weaker answer than a sensitivity table.

A buyer weighing edge against cloud is deciding on four things: what it costs per outcome, where the data sits, what they have to buy, and how often it will be wrong in a way nobody catches. Electricity is not on that list — and the run's own numbers show why it can't be.

Electricity is 2.1% of the local arm's cost and 0.077% of the gap between the two arms. So the question isn't whether the power model is accurate — it's whether any plausible error in it could change an answer:

If the power model is wrong by…Cost per successful recordAdvantage vs cloud-only
nothing$0.0013728.3×
2×$0.0014027.7×
3×$0.0014327.1×
5×$0.0014926.1×

A five-fold error moves the headline from 28× to 26×, against a PRD success target of 5× and a stretch of 15×. Nothing the scorecard decides is sensitive to energy.

Why this is better than deleting the metric

Showing that electricity is a rounding error closes the "but you'll pay for the power" objection. Publishing a tokens-per-joule chart invites it, then loses the argument on a technicality about whose watts you measured. One row and a sensitivity band beats a chart.

What takes the headline slot

Throughput per device — documents per hour per machine, at each concurrency. It sizes a fleet, and fleet size is what gets budgeted. It's the procurement-legible form of the same batching finding the PRD's performance-per-watt table was reaching for, and Pass B already measures it. Only the units of the headline changed; no measurement left the harness.

Energy survives in two places: one line in the cost breakdown shown with the band above, and a binary envelope check — does sustained draw fit the tier's thermal and battery budget? That's a constraint, not a metric. IT blocks inference on active employee laptops regardless of economics, and that's a pass/fail, not a curve.

Why the methodology strip stays, even though it makes the page longer

It is the only part a client's own technical reviewer will read closely, and it is the difference between evidence and a vendor deck. The "not triggered" and "not tested" rows do more work than any chart above them — they show the study could have failed and says where it doesn't apply.

05

What I'd change before building

Promote Cut A into the executive rendering

Cumulative cost with a payback marker is the single most CFO-legible exhibit here, and it costs nothing extra — same views, different axes. It probably belongs at position 3, ahead of the fleet-sizing bar.

Cut C earns its place too

Showing that only document quality moves the answer is what makes the other exhibits credible. It also sets up the ask — send us 30 real invoices — without it sounding like a stall.

The four tiles need your baseline

"14 wrong per 1,000" only lands next to their current manual error rate. That number comes from discovery, not from the harness, and there should be a slot for it in the template.

Exhibit 5 is the sales motion

The degradation curve plus "send us 30 invoices" is a qualification step that costs a day and disqualifies bad-fit prospects early. Worth designing the intake path for it now.

Standing constraint on this template

Every number above is invented. When the harness fills these in for real, the provenance badges and the methodology strip are what stop a projected figure from being read as a measured one in a room where that distinction decides a contract. They are load-bearing, not decoration — do not let a later pass tidy them away for looking cleaner.
